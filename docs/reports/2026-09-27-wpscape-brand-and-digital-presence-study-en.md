## 1. Executive summary

During the last week of September, we at WP Scape reviewed Triovate's company profile, its website and its LinkedIn page, then the Talent Souq web platform and its Android app, and compared all of this with what the market offers today. Our overall impression is that the foundation is solid, and that what the next phase needs is to tidy up and unify the picture rather than build it again from scratch.

The study reached five main findings:

1. **The company has integrated services** in human resources, legal affairs and marketing, and a client list on its website that includes names such as Red Bull, Monin and Fuji Japan — a source of trust that is not easily available.
2. **Triovate presents itself in four different formulations** across its company profile, its website, its LinkedIn page and its communication with us. We believe unifying this formulation comes before any design work.
3. **The two logos meet in the same orange and in the orange dot.** This is a ready-made link that can be built on in the brand family and in the teaser campaign.
4. **Talent Souq has a number of steps to take before it is announced.** The most important: replace the demo data shown on it, secure the domain and email, show Syria in the platform's messaging, and complete the Arabic localization of the app.
5. **We found no platform in the market dedicated to connecting Syrian talent with employers in the Gulf.** In our assessment this is the most suitable space for Talent Souq.

## 2. Scope of the study

We read the current company profile (26 pages), browsed triovate.ae on desktop and mobile — including the "About us", Jobs and Career Center pages — and looked at the data that search engines read from the home page. On LinkedIn we reviewed the description, the logo and the type of content being published.

For Talent Souq, we reviewed the platform at talentsouq.it.com in Arabic and English, then the Android app (version 1.0.0) using the demo employer and job-seeker accounts, in both light and dark modes. We limited ourselves to observation: we submitted no application and published nothing inside the platform. On the market side, we looked at the main recruitment platforms in the Gulf and at what job seekers in Syria use.

## 3. Triovate: positioning and message

For a single company to combine human resources, legal and marketing is an advantage that few in the region have. But the way this trio is expressed changes from one channel to the next:

| Channel | How the company describes itself |
|---|---|
| Company profile | "People. Brand. Digital. The Trio Behind Your Growth." |
| Website | "HR. Digital Marketing. Legal. One partner. Every discipline your business needs to grow." |
| LinkedIn | "Strategic Solutions for Growth, Governance, and Brand Success" |
| Communication with us | "Human resources, recruitment, software, and corporate legal solutions." |

Someone who gets to know the company through two different channels effectively meets two slightly different companies, and it becomes hard for a single image to settle in their mind. This matters even more now, because Talent Souq will add a digital product to this picture for the first time.

We propose adopting one formulation that names what the company actually offers — human resources and recruitment, corporate legal solutions, marketing and technology — and presenting Talent Souq as a **digital extension of the company's recruitment expertise**, not as a fourth service alongside the three. The final wording is best written in a joint working session with your team at the start of the project, because it is an expression of the company before it is marketing copy.

## 4. Triovate: visual identity

The idea of the logo itself is good: columns that take the shape of a person, topped by an orange dot. However, the thin orange columns inside it disappear when the logo is shown small — as a browser icon or on a phone — and the name is set in a generic typeface that leaves no particular impression.

We therefore think the logo should be **refined, not replaced**: its details adjusted so it survives at small sizes; a typeface chosen for the name that suits a consulting firm; and an Arabic typeface adopted alongside it — currently absent, even though most of the audience is Arabic-speaking.

The two colours, navy and orange, are strong and easy to remember; a short guide that fixes the exact values and usage proportions is enough. Imagery is the side that needs the most work: the handshake between human and machine, the wooden cubes and the glowing light bulb are stock-template images more than expressions of Triovate. The audience has seen them in dozens of company profiles, and they pull the identity toward the generic.

## 5. Triovate: digital presence

### Company profile

The cover announces a clear trio, but most of the inner pages go to human resources (thirteen pages), against one page for marketing and one for technology, and no page for corporate legal solutions. The profile does not mention Talent Souq, and it does not show the client logos published on the website, which in our view are among the strongest things the company owns. The profile is available in English only.

Two small points deserve correcting now: the website link on the contact page is written **www.traiovate.ae**, and on the second services page the headings "Job Evaluation" and "Talent Acquisition" appear as plain text rather than as headings.

### Website

The site structure is clear: a path for employers, another for job seekers, a clients section and an existing blog. With the launch of Talent Souq, the "Vacancies" and "CV & Profile Lab" pages will play the same role as the platform, and it is better that they redirect the visitor to it, so that job seekers are not split between two destinations.

We also noticed that the home page title, as search engines read it, presents the company as merely an AI-based recruitment company; that its description still carries default text from the site template ("Elevate your business with Avada Corporation…") and a former company name, **HRB HUB**, which also recurs in one of the client testimonials. The company also lists Arabic and English support among its advantages, while the site itself is in English only.

### LinkedIn

The current page URL (company/hr-legal-&-marketing-solutions-for-gcc-businesses/…) is long and does not carry the company name; it would be better replaced with a short URL bearing the name. Notably, most of what has been published recently is job ads in the UAE, Saudi Arabia and Syria — meaning the page is today acting as a small job board, and its followers are the closest possible audience for Talent Souq in its first days.

## 6. Talent Souq: the web platform

The interface is calm and orderly and serves both sides at once, and the candidate–job match percentage is displayed in an understandable way. The logo — the letters T and S in a single stroke, topped by the orange dot — is simple and works as an app icon, and we see no reason to change it. Its colours are petrol green with the same orange that Triovate uses, and its typefaces are Inter for English and IBM Plex Sans Arabic for Arabic. So far, the platform's connection to the company appears only in the phrase "By Triovate" in the footer.

The Arabic version has the correct direction, but English elements still appear inside it, including the field names (Design · Product · Engineering), the employment type and work mode, and phrases such as "2h ago" instead of "منذ ساعتين".

### Before the teaser campaign starts

- The companies, jobs and numbers currently displayed (500+ opportunities and 120+ companies) are **test data** and should not reach the public with the campaign.
- The platform runs on **talentsouq.it.com**, while the domain **talentsouq.com** is listed for sale with a domain broker. Securing a domain in the brand's name before the announcement is far easier than after the name has spread.
- The privacy policy gives the address **privacy@talentsouq.com**, but that domain is not set up to receive mail; messages sent to it do not arrive.
- The platform speaks of "the Gulf" and names Dubai, Riyadh and Doha, but **does not mention Syria**, although it is part of the first-phase audience.
- There is **no preview image** when the link is shared, so the platform's link appears in WhatsApp and LinkedIn without an image — the first thing the audience will see in the campaign.
- The app is available on Google Play and we did not find it on the Apple App Store; before launch day it needs a **store page with images and text in both languages** on both stores.
- **Complete the Arabic localization** of the interface on both the platform and the app.

## 7. Talent Souq: the mobile app

The app is built as a standalone mobile application, not a copy of the website, and in many respects it is more mature than the web platform. In the employer account we found a daily dashboard, vacancy management, applicant tracking with clear stages, an AI-generated candidate summary and interview questions, alongside assessments, interview scheduling and private notes. In the job-seeker account: quick apply, a match percentage, the skills required for each job, similar jobs, application tracking that allows withdrawing an application, and a profile-completeness indicator.

The design is consistent, dark mode is well executed, and the app is prepared in Arabic for the screen reader used by visually impaired people — a level of care we do not see often. We are pleased that the app shows "by TRIOVATE" under the logo on the welcome screen, and we recommend applying this everywhere.

Nevertheless, while browsing we recorded observations that we think should be addressed before launch:

- The English interface is completely clean, but the Arabic one contains many English phrases, such as: **Talent search · Applicant tracking · Message applicant · Submitted · Not selected · Browse jobs · Find roles now · Preferences · Legal**, in addition to the employment type and closing dates.
- The work mode appears on job cards in its programmatic form **"on_site"** instead of "في الموقع".
- The interview notification shows the appointment in **GMT ("UTC 19:00")**, and the candidate may misunderstand the time.
- The account-creation screen on the job-seeker path shows the phrase **"Hire faster. Post once, reach everyone."**, which is originally addressed to employers.
- The app's tagline on the welcome screen, **"Find Talent. Build Futures."**, differs from the platform's tagline **"Opportunity meets ambition"**; it is better for the product to have a single tagline.
- The app's files occupy about **100 MB** on the device, a noticeable size on Syria's slow networks; it can be reduced by reviewing the components bundled into it.
- The app requests permissions to **display over other apps, the microphone and the camera**; we suggest keeping only what the features actually need, and explaining the reason for each permission to the user when it is requested.
- The app's technical name in the store (**com.karehan.app**) does not carry the brand name, appears in the URL of the app page, and cannot be changed after public release.

## 8. Market and competitors

| Platform | Focus | What distinguishes it |
|---|---|---|
| Bayt.com | General Middle East platform, leaning toward office jobs. | The oldest and most established name in the region. |
| Naukrigulf | The six Gulf states; the UAE is its largest market. | A wide base of expatriate talent from South Asia. |
| GulfTalent | Experienced professionals. | Focus on mid and senior levels. |
| LinkedIn | The global professional network, available in Arabic. | Professional identity and networking; weaker in field jobs. |
| Dubizzle Jobs | UAE, with a strong presence in service and field jobs. | Benefits from the large classifieds site's visitors; a job ad starts at AED 269 before tax. |
| Jadarat (Saudi Arabia) · Nafis (UAE) | Government platforms for citizens. | The official channel for hiring nationals — complementary, not competing. |
| Skillbee | Expatriate labour from South Asia to the Gulf. | The message "no fees for the job seeker". |
| Local platforms in Syria | Such as Forsa, job.sy and WorkLink, plus Telegram channels for job ads. | Close to the local market, but they do not reach employers in the Gulf. |

What caught our attention in this map is that we found **no platform built specifically for the corridor between Syria and the Gulf**, while Triovate already publishes jobs in both directions. The big platforms started in English and were Arabized later, and the field-job platforms mostly speak the languages of South Asia, so an Arabic-first platform remains a nearly empty space. Having a real recruitment team behind Talent Souq, and verified employers, gives a trust that a job board cannot give. Finally, Jadarat and Nafis are dedicated to nationals, so cross-border hiring remains open ground for private platforms.

## 9. Syria: practical considerations for the launch

Internet penetration in Syria reached 35.8% at the start of 2025, and the median mobile download speed was 12.68 Mbps, so the app and the campaign materials must be as light as possible. Apple and Google services have been returning to Syria gradually since 2025, but access still varies from one user to another, so it is best to provide a **direct download link alongside the stores**.

Payment by bank card is still in its infancy, and we do not recommend making a card-paid subscription a condition for Syrian employers in the first phase. Alongside Instagram and LinkedIn, Telegram channels and Facebook groups dedicated to jobs are widespread in Syria, and it is natural for them to be part of the campaign plan there.

## 10. What we recommend

### Brand architecture

We recommend that the platform launches under the name **Talent Souq by Triovate**: it keeps its own identity — the petrol green and the current logo — with a fixed signature that ties it to the parent company. Orange and the dot remain the two elements shared between the two brands. The app already uses the Arabic name **«تالنت سوق»** and the signature **"by TRIOVATE"**; what is needed is to fix these two forms across all channels, not to invent a new one.

### Teaser campaign idea: "The Dot"

The orange dot is the clearest thing the two brands share, which is why we chose it as the axis of the campaign, in four phases:

| Phase | Timing | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Mystery | December | Posts carrying nothing but the dot, with questions the audience knows from experience — "How many CVs have you sent without a reply?" for job seekers, and "How many weeks does hiring take you?" for employers. |
| Reveal | Mid-December | Announce the name and the logo, and open a waiting list for job seekers and employers. |
| Countdown | Second half of December | Introduce the features, announce the "launch partners" among employers, and count down to launch day. |
| Launch | Early January | Publish the download links, invite those registered on the waiting list, and launch the first real jobs. |

We suggest the campaign addresses employers and hiring managers via LinkedIn, making use of the Triovate page's current followers, and reaches job seekers in the Gulf and Syria via Instagram, Facebook and TikTok, using Telegram and WhatsApp to expand the waiting list in Syria.

### Roadmap to launch

We suggest work begins in October with the positioning session, then the brand architecture, the logo refinement and the identity guide, while addressing what is needed on the platform before the teaser campaign. We finish the bilingual company profile and the Instagram and LinkedIn identity before **15 November**, and dedicate the second half of the month to preparing the campaign — concept, calendar, designs and targeting plan. The campaign starts in **December**, and the Talent Souq platform launches at the **start of January 2027**.
